wealth

    Wealth

    Wealth concentrates harder than income. The same country often has a wealth Gini twenty points above its income Gini, and the gap between the two has widened. This is the series the atlas is built on, harmonized from five sources with each row tagged for comparability.

    Wealth Gini

    WID series, per-adult equal-split basis, negative net wealth zeroed.

    Distribution shares

    Top 1%, top 10%, and bottom 50% shares of household net wealth. Draw the Lorenz curve for any country to see the concentration directly.

    Against everyday life

    Take an everyday measure and see it against how concentrated wealth is and how unequal income is. Two scatters, side by side, both correlations shown.

    Income

    Income inequality rose through the 1980s and 1990s in many rich economies, then flattened. Wealth kept concentrating. Ask for the divergence in any country to see both on one chart.

    Income Gini

    World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform, national survey rows: income where surveys measure it, consumption where they measure that instead.

    Median income and GDP

    Median daily income from the same surveys, and GDP per person as the backdrop.

    Wealth against income

    Ask one, then the other, without losing your place: the chain keeps the country and switches the measure. PIP via OWID, CC BY 4.0; sparse surveys draw point by point.

    Wellbeing

    How long people live, how satisfied they say they are, what their economies emit. Each shown against how concentrated wealth is and how unequal income is, side by side, both correlations left to stand.

    Life expectancy

    At birth, both sexes. UN World Population Prospects with Clio Infra and Riley backfill, via Our World in Data.

    Poverty and emissions

    Share below $2.15 a day, and territorial CO2 per person in tonnes, both via Our World in Data.

    Life satisfaction

    The Cantril ladder, 0 to 10, from the Gallup World Poll behind the World Happiness Report.

    The United States, up close

    The same question, one level down. No county has a wealth Gini, so housing stands in for local wealth: home values, and who owns. For mobility, health, and the rich-poor life gap, does a county track its housing wealth or its income inequality? Data from Opportunity Insights, county by county.

    The local wealth signal

    No county has a wealth Gini, so housing stands in for it: homeownership rate and median home value, alongside the county income Gini as the inequality lens.

    What it touches

    Upward mobility for a child raised poor, economic connectedness across class, cohesion, poverty, single-parent share, and where available life expectancy and financial distress.

    Opportunity Insights (the Opportunity Atlas and the Social Capital Atlas with Meta) and the county covariates. County and state level, public and privacy-protected.

      Coverage

      The county data is not loaded yet. Once the Opportunity Insights and Social Capital files are processed into us_county.json, the question box opens here and the measures light up.

      The database

      Five source families in one long panel: WID, the ECB household survey (HFCS), the Luxembourg Wealth Study, the US Survey of Consumer Finances, and the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts. 10,783 rows with a non-null Gini, ISO3 keys, 1800 to 2025 in the release files.

      Comparability flags

      Every row carries comparability_tier (A for harmonized surveys, B for mixed methodology) and top_tail_flag (survey only, or enhanced with administrative data).

      Filtering to like-for-like rows is one line of pandas.

      What the ask tab reads

      The WID Gini series (1990 to 2024, per-adult equal-split, negative wealth zeroed), latest distribution shares by country, and the US long-run series back to 1989.

      Method

      Sources are merged with explicit priority, documented in the methods note. The pipeline is Python and rebuilds the release files from raw inputs.
