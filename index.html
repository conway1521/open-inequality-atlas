How does inequality affect life?

Inequality is called the defining problem of the age. Measured by income, the gap has barely moved in a generation. The one that keeps widening is wealth: the richest 1% of Americans held a fifth of it in 1991 and hold nearly a third now. This atlas takes wealth concentration as the thing to explain, and asks what it does to ordinary life, country by country and, next, county by county.

Your chain

    Wealth

    This is the gap that keeps widening, and the core of the atlas. Wealth accumulates where income only flows, so it concentrates harder: the same country often shows a wealth Gini twenty points above its income Gini, and the distance has grown. Harmonized from five source families with every row tagged for comparability.

    Wealth Gini

    WID series, per-adult equal-split basis, negative net wealth zeroed.

    Distribution shares

    Top 1%, top 10%, and bottom 50% shares of household net wealth. Draw the Lorenz curve for any country to see the concentration directly.

    Against everything else

    The reason the atlas exists: hold life expectancy, poverty, or income up against how unequal a place is, as a scatter or by tier.

    Income

    The gap that stabilized. In many rich economies the income Gini rose through the 1980s and 1990s and has drifted sideways since, even as wealth kept concentrating. That contrast is the point: ask for the divergence in any country to see the two gaps on one chart.

    Wellbeing

    What the wealth gap touches. How long people live, how satisfied they say they are, what their economies burn, next to how concentrated wealth is. At the country average the link is often weak, which is where the real question begins, not ends: the bite shows up closer to the ground. A United States deep-dive by county, built on Opportunity Insights data, is the next tab.

    Life expectancy

    At birth, both sexes. UN World Population Prospects with Clio Infra and Riley backfill, via Our World in Data.

    Poverty and emissions

    Share below $2.15 a day, and territorial CO2 per person in tonnes, both via Our World in Data.

    Life satisfaction

    The Cantril ladder, 0 to 10, from the Gallup World Poll behind the World Happiness Report.

    The database

    Five source families in one long panel: WID, the ECB household survey (HFCS), the Luxembourg Wealth Study, the US Survey of Consumer Finances, and the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts. 10,783 rows with a non-null Gini, ISO3 keys, 1800 to 2025 in the release files.

    Comparability flags

    Every row carries comparability_tier (A for harmonized surveys, B for mixed methodology) and top_tail_flag (survey only, or enhanced with administrative data).

    Filtering to like-for-like rows is one line of pandas.

    What the ask tab reads

    The WID Gini series (1990 to 2024, per-adult equal-split, negative wealth zeroed), latest distribution shares by country, and the US long-run series back to 1989.
